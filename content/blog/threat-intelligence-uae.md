Threat intelligence has a reputation for producing long reports that nobody acts on. For most UAE businesses, the useful version is much simpler: tell me when my brand is being impersonated, when my staff's passwords leak, and when a vulnerability in something I run is being exploited — and tell me what to do about it.

## Brand impersonation

Well-known UAE brands — banks, government services, airlines, delivery companies, real estate developers — are frequently impersonated in phishing campaigns, fake websites, fraudulent social media accounts and scam messages. Customers lose money and trust; the brand takes the reputational damage.

Effective monitoring watches for:

- Newly registered lookalike domains, including Arabic and transliterated variants.
- New TLS certificates issued for domains resembling yours, via certificate transparency logs.
- Fake social profiles and app-store listings.
- Phishing kits that copy your login pages.

Detection is only half the job. Takedown requests to registrars, hosting providers and platforms need evidence and persistence.

## Leaked credentials

Infostealer malware harvests saved passwords and session cookies from infected computers — often personal devices or contractors' laptops — and those logs are sold in bulk. The [Snowflake breaches](/resources/incidents/snowflake-customer-breaches-2024/) showed how years-old stolen credentials can still unlock company data when MFA is missing.

Monitoring criminal markets and breach dumps for your domains lets you reset credentials and revoke sessions before they are used.

## Exploited vulnerabilities

There are tens of thousands of new CVEs each year. A small fraction are actively exploited. CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue, exploit releases and attacker chatter show which ones matter now. Our [CVE advisories](/resources/cve/) cover many that have hit organisations in the region, including flaws in [Fortinet](/resources/cve/cve-2024-21762/), [Palo Alto Networks](/resources/cve/cve-2024-3400/) and [Citrix](/resources/cve/cve-2023-4966/) devices.

## Regional threat context

Gulf organisations face the full range of global cybercrime plus a history of destructive and espionage campaigns against energy, government and critical infrastructure — from [Shamoon](/resources/incidents/saudi-aramco-shamoon-2012/) onwards. Sector-specific briefings help leadership understand which threats are realistic for them and which controls matter most.

## Turning intelligence into action

Every intelligence item should come with an owner and an action: block these indicators, reset these accounts, patch this system, file this takedown. Indicators should flow automatically into your SIEM, EDR and firewalls.
