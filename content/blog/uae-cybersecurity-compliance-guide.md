Ask five Dubai businesses which cybersecurity rules apply to them and you will get five different answers. That is not surprising: the UAE has federal laws, emirate-level regulations, free-zone regimes and sector rules that overlap. This guide maps the main ones and explains how to meet them without running five separate compliance programmes.

*This is general information, not legal advice. Laws and executive regulations change — confirm your obligations with qualified counsel.*

## Federal data protection: the UAE PDPL

Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 on the Protection of Personal Data — the PDPL — is the UAE's general data protection law. In broad terms it:

- Requires a lawful basis, usually consent, for processing personal data.
- Gives individuals rights to access, correct and erase their data and to object to some processing.
- Requires appropriate technical and organisational security measures.
- Requires notification of personal data breaches to the UAE Data Office and, in some cases, to affected individuals.
- Restricts transfers of personal data outside the UAE.

It does not apply in the same way everywhere. Entities in the **DIFC** follow the DIFC Data Protection Law (DIFC Law No. 5 of 2020), and entities in **ADGM** follow the ADGM Data Protection Regulations 2021. Some categories — such as government data and certain health and banking data — are governed by their own legislation.

## Dubai: DESC requirements

The **Dubai Electronic Security Center (DESC)** sets information security requirements for Dubai Government entities, most notably the **Information Security Regulation (ISR)**, and a security standard for cloud service providers serving them. If you supply services to a Dubai Government entity, expect these requirements to appear in your contract and security questionnaires.

## Federal entities and critical infrastructure: UAE IA

The **UAE Information Assurance (IA) Regulation**, originally issued by the National Electronic Security Authority (NESA) and now maintained under the Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority (TDRA), defines management and technical controls for federal entities and critical information infrastructure. Many private organisations use it as a reference baseline even when it is not mandatory for them.

## Sector rules

- **Banking and finance:** institutions licensed by the Central Bank of the UAE follow its requirements on information security, outsourcing and business continuity. DFSA-regulated firms in the DIFC have their own expectations.
- **Healthcare:** Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 on the use of ICT in health fields governs health data, including keeping it inside the UAE, and emirate health authorities set further standards — such as ADHICS in Abu Dhabi.
- **Payments:** card-handling businesses need PCI DSS 4.0 — see [what changed in PCI DSS 4.0](/blog/pci-dss-4-what-changed/).

## International standards customers ask for

Many Dubai companies are asked for **ISO/IEC 27001** certification or a **SOC 2** report by enterprise and international customers, regardless of local law. Our [ISO 27001 certification roadmap](/blog/iso-27001-certification-roadmap/) explains the path.

## One control set, many frameworks

The efficient approach is to build a single, mapped control set — access control, logging, incident response, vendor management, backups, encryption — and show how each control satisfies the PDPL, DESC, UAE IA, ISO 27001 and sector requirements at once. One piece of evidence then serves several audits.

The controls that matter most across every framework are the same ones that stop real attacks: MFA, patching, monitoring, backups and tested incident response.
