Some questions cannot wait for the annual cycle and do not need a full test. An on-demand assessment answers one question, for one decision, by a date.

## Six triggers

| Trigger | Question to answer | Useful scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acquisition | What are we inheriting? | External review, identity, incident history |
| Major release | Is this change safe to ship? | Changed components and authorisation |
| New supplier | Can we trust this connection? | Integration, access and data flow |
| Emergency CVE | Are we affected, and was it used? | Exposure check and compromise hunt |
| Suspicious event | Is there something here? | Targeted triage, then response if needed |
| Audit or tender query | Can we evidence this control? | One control, with proof |

## Write the question first

Test the portal invites a vague report. Can one customer read another customer's documents? can be answered in days, and the reader knows what the answer means.

## Prepare in advance

Speed is lost in paperwork rather than testing. Ahead of time, agree standing rules of engagement, a price basis, confidentiality terms, named approvers, and how test accounts are issued.

## Acquisitions need extra care

Before completion you often lack authority to test the target. Work from public information, documents and interviews, and obtain the owner's written permission for anything active. Test properly after completion, before connecting networks.

## Reading the result

A narrow assessment supports a narrow conclusion. Insist that the report says what was not examined, and do not present it to a customer as a full penetration test. If the question keeps recurring, you need a [continuous programme](/blog/continuous-security-testing-programme/) instead.
