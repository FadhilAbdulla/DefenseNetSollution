When a business in Dubai discovers ransomware on its servers or an attacker in its email, the first day decides most of the outcome. Good decisions in the first hours limit the damage; panicked ones — wiping machines, paying immediately, staying silent — often make it worse.

## The first hour

1. **Do not wipe or rebuild anything yet.** Reinstalling systems destroys the evidence you need to understand how the attacker got in and what they took.
2. **Isolate, do not power off.** Disconnect affected machines from the network if you safely can, but keep them running so memory evidence is preserved.
3. **Protect your backups.** Disconnect backup systems from the network. Attackers routinely target them before deploying ransomware.
4. **Move communications off compromised systems.** If email may be compromised, coordinate by phone or a separate channel.
5. **Call your incident response provider.** If you do not have one, this is the moment it matters most.

## Hours 1 to 24

A professional response team will run three tracks in parallel:

- **Containment:** cutting the attacker's access in a planned order — revoking sessions, resetting privileged credentials, blocking command-and-control traffic and removing persistence.
- **Investigation:** establishing the initial access vector, how far the attacker moved and whether data was stolen. In ransomware cases, exfiltration usually happens before encryption.
- **Business continuity:** deciding what can keep running, what must be restored first, and how to restore onto clean systems rather than reinfecting.

## UAE notification duties

Under the UAE Personal Data Protection Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021), a controller must notify the UAE Data Office of a personal data breach that prejudices the privacy, confidentiality or security of personal data, and in some circumstances inform affected individuals. Organisations in the DIFC and ADGM follow their own data protection regimes, and regulated sectors — banking, health, government — have additional reporting lines.

The legal filing is your counsel's responsibility. Your incident responders' job is to make sure it rests on accurate facts: what data, how many people, when, and how. Check the current executive regulations and sector rules with your legal adviser, because timelines and thresholds matter.

## Ransom payments

Whether to pay is a business, legal and ethical decision, not a technical one. Paying does not guarantee decryption or deletion of stolen data — the [Change Healthcare case](/resources/incidents/change-healthcare-ransomware-2024/) shows how extortion can continue after payment. Sanctions rules may also prohibit payments to certain groups. Make the decision with legal counsel and full information, not under the countdown timer.
